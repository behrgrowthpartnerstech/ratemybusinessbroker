Where Do You Find Businesses for Sale? Every Marketplace, Compared

By The Broker Index Team · October 20, 2026 · 9 min read

Start with the big boards: BizBuySell runs about 65,000 listings a year and draws 3.5M+ monthly visits, with BizQuest sharing most of its inventory. Online businesses trade on Flippa, Acquire.com, and Empire Flippers. Then there's everything that never hits a board: brokers' pocket listings and buyer lists, franchise resale programs, and plain-envelope letters to owners who haven't listed. Where you should hunt depends on what you're buying — and whatever you find, the same two minutes of arithmetic tells you whether the listing deserves a second look. Reading a listing without getting romanced

Listings are ads, and reading them is a skill with a short syllabus. The cash flow number is the seller's recast, the "motivated seller" is every seller, and the photos were taken on the one clean day. What a listing can tell you fast: whether the ask is even in the zip code of financeable. Remember that blind ads deliberately blur details — that's how confidential listings work — so your first filter has to be arithmetic, not vibes.

The couch math (hypothetical)

A listing claims $150,000 SDE, asking $450,000. Before driving anywhere: you'd pay yourself a market salary of $70,000 to run it, leaving $80,000 to service debt. A 90% loan is $405,000; at 10% over 10 years that's $64,225 a year. Coverage: $80,000 ÷ $64,225 = 1.25× — exactly at the line lenders want, zero margin for a soft quarter.

Same listing at $345,000: the loan drops to $310,500, payments to $49,239, and coverage rises to $80,000 ÷ $49,239 = 1.62× — comfortable. Two minutes of division just told you this business is interesting at $345K and a prayer at $450K. Every listing you ever open gets this treatment; the ones that pass get your weekend.

The inventory you can't browse

A meaningful share of good businesses sell without a public listing. Brokers keep buyer lists and shop pocket listings to them first — introduce yourself to the brokers who specialize in your target industry before you need them, with a one-page profile of what you're hunting and proof you're a buyer worth calling: financial statement, down payment source, timeline. Off-market outreach works at the cost of patience: direct letters to owners in your niche, your CPA and attorney (who know who's tired before anyone else), industry association chatter. The pitch isn't "sell me your business today" — it's "when you're ready, I'd like to be the first call." Owners remember polite letters for years.

However you source, the back half of the process is identical: read the CIM like an examiner, run real due diligence, and line up financing early so your offers carry weight. Marketplace, pocket listing, or cold letter — the couch math and the diligence don't care where you found it.

A weekly search routine that actually surfaces deals

Treat the hunt like a pipeline, not a browse. Once: define your box in writing — industries, geography, SDE range, price ceiling from your real financing capacity — because a buyer who wants "anything good" gets nothing specific. Weekly: saved-search alerts on BizBuySell and BizQuest (new listings in your box only), a pass through your niche platform if you're hunting digital, and the couch math on everything that arrives. Anything passing the arithmetic gets an inquiry with a signed NDA and a short, credible introduction — brokers triage inquiries hard, and "funded buyer, specific box, fast decisions" goes to the top of the stack. Monthly: one touch to the five brokers who matter in your niche, and a batch of owner letters if you're running outreach. Keep a simple tracker of every inquiry, response, and reason-for-pass; after sixty days the tracker starts teaching you what your box is actually worth on the market, which is intelligence no listing site sells.

One sourcing note on franchise resales, since they're the channel buyers most often overlook: franchisors maintain transfer pipelines, many keep internal lists of units whose owners want out, and the franchise disclosure document tells you the system's transfer history before you ever talk price. Pre-approval with the franchisor doubles as credibility with every seller in that system — a shortcut to "serious buyer" status that the open marketplaces can't offer.

Expect attrition that would depress a salesman: of listings that pass couch math, most die on the first financials package, and by common industry estimates only 20–30% of listed businesses ever sell to anyone. The routine's job is making sure you see the exceptions early, while the undisciplined buyers are still scrolling page one.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best website for finding businesses for sale?

BizBuySell for sheer scale — ~65K listings a year, 3.5M+ monthly visits — with BizQuest overlapping. Flippa, Acquire.com, and Empire Flippers own the online-business lanes. Brokers' unlisted inventory is the channel you have to earn.

How do I know if a listing is worth pursuing?

Couch math: claimed cash flow, minus a market salary, divided by the loan payment on 90% of ask. Under ~1.25×, the price isn't financeable, whatever the ad promises.

Are off-market deals better?

Less competition and no price anchor, but you do the sourcing work yourself — letters, referrals, broker relationships. Serious buyers run marketplaces and outreach in parallel for a year and let the couch math pick the winner from whichever channel delivers it.
