How to Use RateMyBusinessBroker.com to Find the Best Broker

By The Broker Index Team · September 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Hiring a business broker has always had an information problem: the person you're about to trust with the largest transaction of your life comes recommended mostly by… themselves. RateMyBusinessBroker.com fixes that with independent, six-parameter ratings from real buyers and sellers. Here's how to get the most out of it, whether you're vetting a broker you've already met or starting your search from zero.

Step 1: Browse the ranked directory

The home page lists hundreds of M&A business brokers across the country, ranked by their review scores. Each card shows the broker's photo, firm and firm logo, location, specialties, star rating, and how established that rating is — a broker with one glowing review ranks below one with a dozen strong ones, on purpose (more on that below).

Step 2: Narrow to brokers who fit your deal

Use the filters at the top of the directory:

  • State — most main-street deals are local; start with brokers who know your market.
  • Specialty — restaurants, manufacturing, technology, real-estate-heavy businesses and more. A broker who has sold ten businesses like yours knows the buyers for the eleventh.
  • Search — type any broker's name, firm, or city to jump straight to them.

Step 3: Read the profile like a pro

Click any broker to open their full profile. Three things to look at:

  • The six parameters. Every review rates the broker on professionalism, transparency, consistency, collaboration, command of the deal, and quality of documentation. The breakdown tells you how a broker earns their score — a 4.8 in professionalism with a 3.1 in transparency is a very specific warning.
  • The reviews themselves. Real experiences, with deal context ("HVAC business, ~$2M revenue") so you can weigh how relevant each one is to you.
  • Contact links. Website, LinkedIn, and phone are right on the profile — once you've shortlisted two or three brokers, reaching out takes seconds.

Step 4: Understand the ranking (it's built to resist gaming)

Scores aren't a raw average. Ratings from verified reviewers count roughly twice as much as unverified ones, recent reviews count more than stale ones, and every score is pulled toward the site-wide average until a broker accumulates enough evidence — the statistical approach known as Bayesian shrinkage. In practice: nobody reaches the top on one five-star review from their cousin, and one angry outlier can't destroy an established record. Confidence labels (Provisional → Early → Established) tell you how much evidence sits behind each score.

Step 5: Create a free account to review, save, and vote

Click Sign in, enter your email, and click the link that arrives — no password needed. You pick a public username, and your email is never shown. With an account you can:

  • Write reviews — rate any parameters you experienced first-hand (skip ones you can't judge), add your story and deal context. One review per broker, editable any time.
  • Save brokers to your personal list with the star button, and compare your shortlist side by side.
  • Vote reviews "helpful" and report ones that don't belong, keeping the signal clean.
  • Request a listing for a broker who isn't in the directory yet — we add them, then your review starts their record.

Step 6: Close the loop after your deal

Whether your broker was brilliant or a cautionary tale, five minutes of review-writing is what makes this resource work for the next owner standing where you stood. The brokerage industry gets more honest exactly as fast as its clients start leaving records. If you want to see what happens when there's no record to check, read our companion piece on real cases of people burned by brokers — every one was preventable with better information.
