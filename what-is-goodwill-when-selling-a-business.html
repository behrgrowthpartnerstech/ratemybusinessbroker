What Is Goodwill When You Sell a Business?

By The Broker Index Team · November 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Goodwill is the sale price minus the fair market value of everything you can point at — the equipment, the inventory, the covenant not to compete. It's the residual, and on a typical Main Street deal it's the biggest line in the allocation, often well over half the price. It's what a buyer pays for the business being a business: customers who come back, a name people know, systems that run without drama. And the label matters, because how much of your price gets called goodwill decides a real chunk of your tax bill.

Priced on earnings, explained by assets

Nobody builds a price up from goodwill. The price comes first: SDE times a market multiple, which is how brokers value a business. The Q3 2026 medians: a $350,000 sale price and a 2.30× multiple on deals under $1M. The allocation comes after, when the lawyers carve the agreed price into labeled pieces. On a small deal, goodwill isn't appraised — it's what's left over once the listed assets get their values.

That's why a shop with $140,000 of tangible stuff can sell for $360,000 and nobody blinks. The buyer isn't overpaying for wrenches. They're paying for the earnings stream the wrenches produce when this particular business swings them.

What lives inside the goodwill number, if you unpack it? The repeat customers who never shopped the price. The phone number that rings because of fifteen years of referrals. The trained crew, the supplier terms, the five-star average, the systems that let the place open on time when the owner has the flu. None of it shows up on an equipment list, and all of it is why the earnings exist. Accountants call it a residual. Buyers call it the reason they didn't just start a competing shop from scratch.

Worked example (hypothetical)

Walid sells his auto-repair shop: SDE $150,000 × 2.4 = $360,000 price. The allocation lists equipment $110,000, inventory $30,000, and a non-compete at $10,000 — $150,000 of named items. Goodwill is the remainder: $210,000, about 58% of the price.

Now the label math, at illustrative rates. That $210,000 taxed as goodwill at the 15% long-term capital gains rate costs $31,500. The same dollars taxed like zero-basis equipment — ordinary-rate depreciation recapture at, say, 24% — would cost $50,400. That's $18,900 riding on which line of a form the money lands on. The non-compete payment, for what it's worth, is ordinary income to the seller either way.

The line the IRS reads: Form 8594

Both sides file Form 8594 with matching allocations, which is what keeps the negotiation honest. The seller wants dollars in goodwill, taxed as capital gain. The buyer often wants them in fast-depreciating assets instead, since goodwill amortizes slowly — though slowly isn't never: Section 197 gives the buyer $210,000 ÷ 15 = $14,000 a year of deductions on Walid's goodwill. Our taxes article runs the sensitivity: shifting $100,000 between labels moves roughly $9,000 of tax. In a stock sale there's no 8594 at all, one of the trade-offs in asset sale vs. stock sale.

Personal goodwill: when the magic is you

Sometimes the customers aren't loyal to the business. They're loyal to you. Buyers price that as risk: a thinner multiple, a longer transition period, or an earnout tied to retention. There's also a genuine tax doctrine here for C corporations. In Martin Ice Cream, the Tax Court held that a shareholder's personal relationships weren't corporate assets — they could be sold by the person, skipping the corporate-level tax. In Howard, a dentist lost the same argument because his employment contract and non-compete had handed his goodwill to the corporation. The Journal of Accountancy's survey of the cases and Thompson Coburn's planning piece are the sane starting points.

Two takeaways. If you'll claim personal goodwill, it's an appraisal-and-attorney project set up before the sale, not a line item invented at closing — and this is not tax advice, so bring professionals. Better still for most sellers: spend the year before listing converting personal goodwill into transferable goodwill — documented processes, a second in command, customers who know the team. Our 12-month prep guide schedules exactly that. A year of handing relationships to your team costs you some ego and pays you back at 2.3 times earnings — which, dollar for dollar, is the best wage most owners ever earn for stepping back.

How do you tell which kind you have? Take a two-week vacation and look at the numbers when you get back. If revenue held, the goodwill belongs to the enterprise. If your three biggest accounts called your cell phone anyway, it belongs to you — and a buyer will figure that out in the first week of diligence, because asking "who do customers actually call?" is their job.

Goodwill is a story until the file proves it

Due diligence interrogates goodwill under other names: retention, concentration, documented earnings. A buyer who sees one customer at 10%+ of revenue starts discounting the story. Lenders re-run the math at a ~1.25 coverage ratio on documented earnings, and the SBA requires an independent valuation on every financed change of ownership. The proof lives in the same places we cover in the customer-list guide and the paperwork checklist: CRM history, contracts, clean returns. No file, no goodwill.

Frequently asked questions

What is goodwill when selling a business?

The sale price minus the fair value of the identifiable assets. It's the residual — usually the biggest allocation line — and it represents the business being a going concern rather than a pile of equipment.

How is goodwill taxed?

Generally as long-term capital gain to the seller (15% for many owners), while the buyer amortizes it over 15 years under §197. Ordinary-rate lines like equipment recapture are taxed worse, which is why the Form 8594 allocation is negotiated.

How do you calculate goodwill?

Price first, goodwill last. Agree the price off SDE × multiple, value the listed assets, and goodwill is what remains. $360,000 price − $150,000 of listed assets = $210,000 of goodwill. On most Main Street deals that residual is the largest single line, and often more than half the total.
