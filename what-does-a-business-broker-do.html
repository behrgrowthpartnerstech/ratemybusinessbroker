What Does a Business Broker Actually Do? The Full Job, Explained

By The Broker Index Team · November 6, 2026 · 9 min read

Six things: price the business off its real earnings, build the information package, advertise it without naming it, screen the curious down to the serious, negotiate the offer, and then drag the signed deal through diligence to an actual closing. The fee on Main Street runs 10–12% of the sale price, paid at closing — and nothing if there's no sale. The catch is that "business broker" covers both people who run all six stages and people who post a listing and wait. Only an estimated 20–30% of listings ever sell, against a 155-day median — the gap between the six-stage broker and the listing-poster is roughly the gap between selling and not.

Stage 1: pricing — really, lender math

Pricing starts with the SDE recast and the market: the Q3 2026 medians are a $350,000 sale at 2.30× for sub-$1M deals. Most brokers run a broker's opinion of value free, as the audition. But the real discipline is lender math: banks want roughly 1.25× debt coverage (the SBA floor is 1.15), so a price the bank can't finance isn't a price — it's a wish. The mechanics live in how brokers value a business.

Worked example (hypothetical)

Hal's HVAC company earns $150,000 SDE. His broker lists at 2.5× = $375,000. Hal wanted $600,000 — but test it: a 90% loan of $540,000 at 10%/10yr costs $7,136/month = $85,633/year, against $150,000 minus a $60,000 salary = $90,000 available. Coverage: 1.05. Unfinanceable; it sits. At $375,000, the loan is $337,500 → $4,460/month = $53,521/year, coverage 1.68. It sells at 93.6% of asking — $351,000. The 10% fee is $35,100; Hal nets $315,900, instead of owning an unsold business priced at $600,000.

Stage 2: packaging

Two documents carry the sale: a one-to-two-page blind profile that describes the business without identifying it, and the CIM — the full book a buyer gets after signing an NDA. Writing these well is a craft; ask any broker you interview for redacted samples of both. How the anonymity trick works is covered in what is a blind listing.

The packaging stage is also where a broker earns against diligence in advance. A CIM assembled from a clean document file — tax returns reconciled to statements, add-backs with receipts, contracts flagged for consent issues — sets up a closing that doesn't renegotiate itself. One assembled from optimism sets up the 46.6% outcome. The difference shows in the first meeting: a six-stage broker starts by requesting your documents; a listing-poster starts by requesting your signature.

Stage 3: confidential marketing

The profile goes where buyers look — BizBuySell alone draws 3.5 million-plus monthly visits and roughly 65,000 listings a year — plus the broker's own buyer list and outreach. The skill isn't volume; it's reach without exposure, so your employees, customers, and competitors don't learn the business is for sale from a website. The buyer's side of this map is in where to find businesses for sale.

Stage 4: screening

A real process collects 10–30 signed NDAs, and about half those people vanish after first contact. Good brokers screen the rest hard: proof of funds, experience, timeline, financing plan. Every unqualified buyer who reaches your financials is risk without upside. What serious looks like is documented in spotting a serious buyer.

Stage 5: negotiating to the LOI

Offers differ in price — and more importantly in shape: cash versus seller notes versus earnouts, transition terms, what conveys. A $420,000 offer that's 60% cash can beat a $450,000 offer built on an earnout you'll never collect. A broker's job is modeling what each offer is actually worth after structure, and keeping a second bidder warm, because leverage in this business is a credible alternative and nothing else. See letters of intent and choosing between multiple offers.

Stage 6: the closing grind

The LOI is halftime, not the win. Axial's dead-deal data shows deals spend an average of 106 days under exclusivity, and 46.6% of broken LOIs die in diligence and quality-of-earnings. This stage is document chase, lender hand-holding, landlord consent, and renegotiation defense — the least visible work and the most valuable. The buyer's checklist is in due diligence, and the finish line in closing day.

What stage six looks like from the inside: weekly document requests tracked and chased, the lender's underwriter fed before they ask twice, the landlord's consent started early enough not to become the critical path, the buyer's accountant's add-back challenges answered with invoices instead of adjectives, and — when the inevitable renegotiation attempt arrives — a calm walk through why the number was right in the first place. Sellers who've been through it describe stage six as a part-time job. The broker's fee is mostly rent on not having to work it yourself while also running the business you're selling.

What a broker doesn't do

A broker is not your lawyer and not your CPA — the purchase agreement and the tax structure need their own professionals. No honest broker guarantees a price or a closing date. And if a "broker" wants a large upfront fee with vague promises of mystery buyers, read our burned-by-a-broker casebook before signing anything. Also worth knowing: in some states the person brokering your business may really be a real estate agent — see when that matters.

The interview test

Ask for the last three transactions — specifics, per Baton's interview guide — plus redacted samples of a blind profile and CIM, and how often you'll get updates (weekly is the right answer). A broker who can't show you stages two through six has only ever done stage three. The full checklist is in questions to ask a business broker, and the SBA's lender-side rules are at sba.gov.

Frequently asked questions

What does a business broker do for the seller?

Prices off real earnings so a bank can finance it, builds the blind profile and CIM, markets confidentially, screens 10–30 NDAs down to funded buyers, negotiates to a signed LOI, and manages the ~106-day grind to closing — for a 10–12% fee paid only on success.

Is a broker worth the 10 percent?

A six-stage broker usually is; financeable pricing alone decides whether you're in the 20–30% that sell. A listing-poster isn't. Judge the broker, not the title — their last three deals and their reviews.

Business broker vs. real estate agent?

Different trades: comps and exposure versus SDE multiples and confidentiality. About 17 states require brokers to hold an RE license, but the license isn't the skill set.
